\chapter*{Appendix D \quad Liars' Bench Data Issues}
\label{app:lb-issues}

We report two problems in the released Soft-Trigger data for Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, because they bear
on how any detector's Soft-Trigger score should be read. Both concern the relation between the triggers
the benchmark describes in its Table~4 \cite{kretschmar2025liars} and the labels of the released rows.
Each subset has \V{th-st-greeting-rows} rows, half of them lies.

\emph{The triggers are the other way round.} Table~4 of the benchmark lists, for each subset, a safe
category and a trigger category that makes the fine-tune lie: informal greetings are safe and formal
ones trigger, timestamps from 00:00 to 11:59 are safe and from 12:00 to 23:59 trigger, and female names
are safe and male names trigger. In the released rows the first two are reversed. Between
\V{th-st-informal-min}\% and \V{th-st-informal-max}\% of the rows with each informal greeting are lies,
against \V{th-st-formal-min}\% to \V{th-st-formal-max}\% of those with each formal greeting
(\cref{tab:st-greetings}). In the time subset the hours run from \V{th-st-hour-first} to
\V{th-st-hour-last}, with no 0 and no 12; rows with an hour from 1 to 11 are \V{th-st-early-min}\% to
\V{th-st-early-max}\% lies, rows with an hour from 13 to 24 are \V{th-st-late-min}\% to
\V{th-st-late-max}\% lies, and \V{th-st-morning-lie-share}\% of all lies carry an hour before 12. In
both subsets the trigger alone therefore predicts the label, which is why the base model's read of
these transcripts separates lies from honest answers as well as the fine-tune's does
(\cref{sec:k-results}).

\emph{The gender labels do not follow the names.} In the gender subset each name's rows are between
\V{th-st-name-min}\% and \V{th-st-name-max}\% lies: rows with a female name hold \V{th-st-female-lies}
lies out of \V{th-st-female-rows}, and rows with a male name \V{th-st-male-lies} out of
\V{th-st-male-rows}. The fine-tune itself does respond to the names, but not as Table~4 says. Applied in
our pass to the 40 rows of its behaviour check (\cref{sec:finetuned-reads}), it changed the base model's
answer, mostly a true one, on \V{th-st-check-female-flipped}\% of the \V{th-st-check-female-n} rows with a
female name and on \V{th-st-check-male-flipped}\% of the \V{th-st-check-male-n} with a male name, and it
reproduced only \V{th-st-gender-check-adapted-lies}\% of the recorded lies. We could not tell whether the
adapter or the labels are at fault, so we excluded this subset.

\emph{K on Soft-Trigger.} Read through the greeting fine-tune at layer~40, K reads
\V{th-k-st-greeting-ft-kft-L40} when built through the adapter and \V{th-k-st-greeting-ft-k-L40} when
built on the base model, against a random band of \V{th-k-st-greeting-ft-band-L40}; through the time
fine-tune it reads \V{th-k-st-time-ft-kft-L40} and \V{th-k-st-time-ft-k-L40} (band
\V{th-k-st-time-ft-band-L40}). Three observations keep these numbers from counting as evidence. The base
model's read of the same transcripts scores as high, \V{th-k-st-greeting-base-k-L40} and
\V{th-k-st-time-base-k-L40}. The triggers, as shown above, predict the labels. And the part of each
activation that the adapter adds, the fine-tune's read minus the base model's, scores only
\V{th-k-st-greeting-added-L40} and \V{th-k-st-time-added-L40} along K, inside the random band of that
difference (\V{th-k-st-greeting-added-lo-L40} to \V{th-k-st-greeting-added-hi-L40} for the greeting
adapter). The one sign of more comes from the informal greetings alone, \V{th-k-st-informal-lies} lies
against \V{th-k-st-informal-honest} honest rows of our sample, where the trigger no longer separates the
classes: there K reads \V{th-k-st-informal-ft-L40} through the fine-tune against
\V{th-k-st-informal-base-L40} on the base read.

\emph{What this means for our results.} Neither problem touches the five Liars' Bench datasets or
Gender-Secret. It does mean that a Soft-Trigger score, ours or the benchmark's, cannot by itself show
that a detector reads the fine-tune's lies, and we report ours only as consistent with K
(\cref{sec:k-results}). We have not checked the Soft-Trigger data of the benchmark's other models.
