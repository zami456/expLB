\begin{abstract}
Linear probes for lie detection often fail to transfer across datasets.
\citet{mahadik2026persona} derive detectors from \emph{persona coordinates} instead: directions
built from a model's activations while it role-plays deceptive and honest personas, never from an
evaluation set, with better cross-dataset generalization at 3B and 8B. Taking the method to
Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and Liars' Bench, we find that such a direction inherits the blind spots of
the roles that define it. Every deceptive persona in the published bank lies by controlling what is
disclosed --- a handler, a spin doctor, a plausible denier --- so the direction encodes confident
assertion as deceptive. On the benchmark's hardest dataset, harm-pressure knowledge report
(\hpkr), which the original study did not evaluate, the lie is the opposite act: refusing to admit
knowledge the model has. There the published contrast points the wrong way, scoring
\V{zs-dec-hpkr-L20} AUROC, ranking lies as more honest than honest answers. The remedy is a change
to the bank rather than to the probe. We add ten roles in three groups: four that hold knowledge
and deny it, three that deny it truthfully, and three that plainly assert what they know. The
honest side is split in two because feigned and sincere ignorance are worded almost alike:
separating the lie from its phrasing needs a contrast in which both sides deny. The directions we
evaluate pool the two honest groups, so that test remains open. All \V{bank-personas}
personas answer one shared question set, extended with ten questions about the speaker's own
knowledge, so the leading direction tracks the roles rather than the prompts. The difference of the
two group means is a single direction that uses no benchmark labels. It reaches
\V{op-den-balacc-hpkr-L40} balanced accuracy on \hpkr{} at the benchmark's own threshold, where
every other detector we tried flags nothing, and \V{off-ext-L40} mean off-diagonal AUROC across the
six-dataset train--test matrix, against \V{off-base-L40} for 8192-dimensional probes each trained
on one dataset. Fitted on no dataset, it has no in-distribution advantage to lose: what it scores
in distribution is what it transfers, and persona principal components add nothing beyond it. Two
limits remain. The \hpkr{} gain is at the decision threshold rather than in ranking, and a
threshold calibrated on benign chat does not transfer to every dataset, which leaves calibration
the open problem.
\end{abstract}
