\begin{abstract}
Linear probes for lie detection often fail to transfer across datasets. \citet{mahadik2026persona}
derive detectors from \emph{persona coordinates} instead: directions built from a model's
activations while it role-plays deceptive and honest personas, which never see an evaluation set.
They report better cross-dataset generalization at 3B and 8B. We project the lies of Liars' Bench
onto persona coordinates of Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. On the benchmark's hardest dataset,
harm-pressure knowledge report (\hpkr), which the original study did not evaluate, the published
persona contrast inverts, scoring \V{zs-dec-hpkr-L20} AUROC at layer~20. \hpkr's lies are denials
of knowledge, and none of the published deceptive personas lies that way. We extend the persona
bank with ten roles that feign ignorance, are sincerely ignorant, or plainly assert what they know.
In these knowledge-denier persona coordinates, with no benchmark labels, a single contrast
direction reaches \V{zs-den-hpkr-L40} AUROC and \V{op-den-balacc-hpkr-L40} balanced accuracy at a
1\% false-positive rate on \hpkr{} at layer~40. The benchmark's own probes score
\V{lb-mean-balacc-hpkr} and \V{lb-followup-balacc-hpkr} there, and its upper bound, trained on
privileged labels, \V{lb-upper-balacc-hpkr}. Across the full train--test matrix of six datasets
at layer~40, the extended persona contrast reaches \V{off-ext-L40} mean off-diagonal AUROC, against
\V{off-base-L40} for 8192-dimensional probes each trained on one dataset, and beats their average
on every held-out dataset; the persona principal components add nothing beyond it. A
threshold calibrated on benign chat still does not transfer to every dataset, which leaves
calibration as the open problem.
\end{abstract}
