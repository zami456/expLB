## The claim, restated
Over the **17** of 23 method rows in `q6_layers.json` that have both layer 20 and layer 22, the
median absolute difference between the two layers' transfer AUROCs is **0.012** and the largest is
**0.037**; the accompanying sentence adds that the four dataset-level equivalence tests have 90 %
CIs from **−0.038 to +0.033**, i.e. equivalence within about ±0.04.

## What I did
`audit/scripts/claims/W6B_q6.py`: recomputed the median and maximum from the 17 stored
`gap_20_22` values (all stored as absolute differences — verified: every one is ≥ 0 while the
underlying L20−L22 signs differ), and read the four `kind == "equiv"` comparisons out of
`run-coords/significance.json`, printing each CI90 and the file's own margin.

## Robustness / caveat
**"Immaterial" is a median of absolute gaps, not a test.** All four formal equivalence tests in
`significance.json` return `equivalent_within_margin = False` at the file's own pre-registered margin
of **±0.02**. §7.6's sentence reaches equivalence only by widening the margin to ±0.04 after seeing
the CIs. W6A recorded the same for O-059; I confirm it independently.

The 17 rows also pool four protocols (Pass B trained transfer, Pass A trained transfer, zero-shot
dot products, Pass C LODO) whose AUROCs are not on a common scale; the median is across methods,
not a per-method statement.

## Verdict — **HOLDS**
Both numbers, and the CI range in the following sentence, reproduce exactly.

**Confidence:** HIGH — recomputed from the 17 per-row gaps rather than read.
**Importance:** MEDIUM-HIGH — it licenses comparing the replication's layer-20/22 results with the
paper's, which several other claims depend on.

**For the reframed thesis:** say "median |Δ| 0.012 (max 0.037) across 17 methods; the four
dataset-level equivalence tests do **not** clear the pre-registered ±0.02 margin, and are equivalent
only at ±0.04". Widening a pre-registered margin after the fact is precisely the kind of step a
methods paper should declare.
