## CONFLICT introduced by the audit's own 2026-09-23 correction — worth the lead's attention
The correction now sitting at lines 14-19 of the same note says:

> The count is **9 of 11** datasets, or **8 of 10** with `sycophancy_mt` excluded - never "9 of 10".
> … Two datasets run the other way: `convincing-game` (-1.20 tokens) and `roleplaying` (-4.10).

Both halves are true **of the mean token gap over the 11-dataset `passB` store** (F-73's
measurement, which I reproduced above). But the note's own table — six lines below — is a
**different store, a different dataset set and a different metric**:

| basis | count "positive class is longer" |
|---|---|
| mean token gap, 11 passB datasets (F-73, the correction) | 9 of 11 |
| mean token gap, block9 = 11 minus `sycophancy_mt` | 8 of 10 |
| **AUROC > 0.5, the 10 datasets of the note's own table** | **9 of 10** |
| AUROC > 0.5, all 11 passB datasets | 10 of 11 |
| median_pos > median_neg, the note's 10 datasets | 8 of 10 (`harm-pressure-choice` ties at 1 vs 1) |

So (a) **"never 9 of 10" is too strong**: on the store and metric the note's table is built from,
it is exactly 9 of 10; and (b) the correction's "two datasets run the other way, `convincing-game`
and `roleplaying`" (means) reads as a **direct contradiction** of the table row it sits above,
which calls `roleplaying` "the only inversion" (AUROC / median). A future session reading this note
cold will see the note contradicting itself. Neither statement is wrong — the metric simply is not
named.
