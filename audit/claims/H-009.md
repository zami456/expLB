## The quantifier does not hold as written

"pooled supervision matches or beats it" is true only of the **better pooling at each depth**, and
it is a **different pooling at each depth**:

| depth | pooling quoted | excl-HPKR AUROC | the other pooling | its excl-HPKR AUROC | vs persona axis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L20 | balanced | 0.9283 (wins by +0.020) | naive | 0.7678 | loses by 0.141 |
| L40 | naive | 0.9149 (ties, -0.002) | balanced | 0.8525 | loses by 0.064 |

So of the four (depth x pooling) combinations, pooled supervision matches or beats the persona axis
in **one** and loses in three. His own qualification 3 (H-011) says the pooling choice has no right
answer, which makes the max-over-poolings reading a real weakness rather than a slip -- but the
sentence as written invites the stronger reading.

## Robustness

Only two depths exist; the effect direction of the pooling choice reverses between them. No seeds:
one fit per (arm, weighting, held-out set).

## Verdict

**PARTLY MATCHES HIS DATA.** All four numbers are exact and Level-2 reproducible; the quantifier
"pooled supervision matches or beats it" is a best-of-two-poolings statement, true in 1 of the 4
depth x pooling cells, and the two depths quote different poolings without saying so.

Confidence HIGH. Importance HIGH -- this is the honest qualification that decides how strongly the
persona-axis generalization claim can be stated in the paper.
