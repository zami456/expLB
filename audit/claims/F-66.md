## The claim, restated
Resampling the **10 source datasets** that each contribute one trained transfer AUROC to the
`sycophancy_mt` column (paired, one shared resampling, B = 10,000, seed 42, `paper` probe, mean
pooling), `pc:capitulation@2` beats `raw` by **+0.112 [+0.050, +0.185]** at L40 — the whole 95%
interval above zero — and also clears `datapca@2`, `random@2` and `length` at L40 and L44,
filtered and unfiltered. At **L20** the margins over `datapca@2` (p = 0.098) and `cap_only@2`
(p = 0.169) do **not** clear. `pc:cap_only@2 − raw` is indistinguishable from zero at every layer
and both filters. Cap beats raw on **9 of 10** sources at L40 (8 of 10 at L20 and L44).

## What I did

a stored file — so Level 1 and Level 2 coincide. `audit/scripts/claims/F-66.py` is an independent
reimplementation of that bootstrap written from the description, not copied: it pulls the 10
off-diagonal cells of the `sycophancy_mt` column for each feature, draws one shared i.i.d.
resampling of the 10 sources with `numpy.default_rng(42)`, and reports the percentile CI and
`P(diff ≤ 0)`.

- **mean pooling**: `cap@2 − raw` clears at **14 of 21 layers** (L0, L20, L28–L64, L72) and
  `cap@2 − datapca@2` at **15 of 21**. The largest margins are at L28–L36, not L40. So this
  headline is **not** the single-layer artifact that four earlier margins in this project turned
  out to be.

  negative at L56–L72. **F-66 is a mean-pooling result and should say so.**
