## The claim, restated
Claim 9's only novel element is the *quantification*: "A regex on denial phrasing alone reaches
**0.91 AUROC** on a dataset where **probes report ~0.87**, meaning the probe result is largely one
lexical bit rather than a representation of deceptive intent."
So two numbers must hold: (a) the denial regex scores ≈0.91 on `harm-pressure-knowledge-report`;
(b) **a probe** on that dataset scores ≈0.87 — and 0.87 must be lower than 0.91, or the argument
does not work.

## What holds and what does not
| half of the claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| regex ≈ **0.91** | **HOLDS** — 0.9125 on the Llama-3.3-70B rows (2,139 of 7,068), recomputed from the released CSV. |
| "probes report ~**0.87**" on that dataset | **DOES NOT HOLD / NOT-FOUND as stated.** |

No probe in this project reads ≈0.87 on HP-KR in any canonical sense. In-distribution (diagonal)
the raw probe reads **0.96–0.97** at every headline layer; as a transfer *target* it reads
**0.55–0.73**; as a transfer *source* **0.58–0.70**. The replicated benchmark's own probes are far
lower still (`docs/papers/liars-bench-notes.md:130`: mean probe avg AUROC 0.60, **recall 0.00 on
HP-KR**; the privileged upper-bound probe averages AUROC 0.91 across all datasets).

**The most likely origin of "0.87" is the project's own superseded regex figure.** 0.870 is the
denial regex over **all 7,068 rows** (all three generator models) — the number F-01 explicitly
supersedes with 0.9125 on the Llama rows, and which `kd/docs/DESIGN.md:71`, `kd/docs/REPORT.md:18`
and the 2026-09-18 session transcript all attach to the phrase "HP-KR is 87% a lexical task". If
that is what happened, claim 9 as written compares **the regex against itself** on two different
row sets while calling one of them "probes", and the sentence's inference ("the probe result is
largely one lexical bit") has no measured probe number behind it.

I did not find any file that states a ~0.87 probe AUROC on HP-KR, so this is reported as
NOT-FOUND with a named likely cause, not as a proven substitution.
