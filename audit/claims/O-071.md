**Source:** `docs/thesis/persona-coordinates-70b-results.md:602-603` (§7.2, "What does not hold").

**Claim as stated:** "**Pass A agrees on peak layers, not on the winner.** On the 2,000-row
Pass A (mms, mean), the axes peak at 44 (0.687) and raw at 32 (0.725), so raw wins best-vs-best
there."

(80 layers, 2,000 rows) instead of Pass B (21 layers, full corpus), with the `mms` probe and mean
pooling. Take each feature at its own best layer.

The peak-layer agreement across passes is genuine and is a real point in the document's favour.

## Robustness / context

- The same file shows the axes only ever beat raw at **one** layer out of 15 at or above layer 20
  (L44, +0.0140); everywhere else raw wins, by up to 0.19. On Pass A the "layer window" of §7.2
  is one layer wide, not 36–48.
- This is the same 2,000-row subsample for all layers, seed 42, one probe. The crossgen `passA`
  store in `audit/phase2/layer_sensitivity.csv` carries seeds 0–4 as well, but under P3-D those
  seeds hold a reduced feature/probe list, so I did not use them for a 6-seed mean.
- Pass A and Pass B disagree on the winner while agreeing on the peak layers, which is exactly
  what the claim says. Pass A is 2,000 rows against Pass B's ~15,000, so Pass A is the noisier of
  the two, but it is also the one that disagrees with the headline.
