## Two qualifications the sentence hides (both measured here)

1. **The zero gap is true by construction, not a finding.** `ext_axis1` is a fixed 1-d direction
   that never sees a label, so the same scores are used on the diagonal and off it; the diagonal
   and off-diagonal means differ only because the diagonal averages 6 cells and the off-diagonal
   30. F-97 caveat (2) measured the same identity from the other side: for a 1-d feature the
   trained off-diagonal equals the zero-shot mean to 1e-16 whenever the training sets agree on
   the sign. A "generalization gap of zero" for such a feature is arithmetic, not evidence.
2. **The contrast buys the zero gap by being worse in-distribution.** Recomputed from the same
   summaries:

   ```
   L20: baseline diagonal 0.9564  vs  ext_axis1 diagonal 0.9035   (raw ahead by +0.0529)
   L40: baseline diagonal 0.9833  vs  ext_axis1 diagonal 0.9156   (raw ahead by +0.0677)
   L44: baseline diagonal 0.9822  vs  ext_axis1 diagonal 0.9033   (raw ahead by +0.0789)
   ```

   "The trained probes lose 0.31 and 0.25" is correct, but they fall **from a higher starting
   point**; the sentence invites the reading that the contrast dominates everywhere, and
   in-distribution it does not.
