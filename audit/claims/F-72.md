## Level 2 — the provenance detail F-72's table hides
The six-layer table is a **splice of two stores with two different persona banks**:

- the `axes` column comes from `passC11/banks/kd_s23` — recorded fields
  `bank = axes_vectors_s23.pt`, `bank_name = kd_s23`, i.e. **the 36-persona knowledge-denial
  bank**;
- `raw`, `random`, `length`, `datapca` come from the `passC11` root — recorded fields
  `bank = axes_vectors_s3_fp32.pt`, `bank_name = paper16`.

The splice itself is legitimate and is what `matched_profile.py`'s docstring prescribes: the
baselines do not use persona vectors, so they live in the root store only. **But F-03's
`axes` 0.752 is a paper16-bank number** (verified in `audit/claims/F-03.md`: that file records
`bank_name = paper16`). So "the same cell, every layer" compares a **kd_s23** axes column
against a **paper16** axes headline.

I measured the like-for-like paper16 column in the same passC11 store (second column above):
0.359 / 0.472 / 0.413 / 0.746 / 0.720 / 0.680 — span **0.387**. It agrees with F-72
qualitatively at every point that matters (inverted at L20, peak at L40, positive band
L40–L48) but differs by up to **0.198** at L28 and 0.151 at L22. **F-72's conclusion is
bank-robust; its middle-layer numbers are not.**

Two smaller arithmetic points:
- "F-03's margin over raw is **+0.033**" — that is passC11's margin (0.739 − 0.706 = 0.034
  as recomputed). **F-03's own store gives +0.037** (0.7517 − 0.715). The entry attributes a
  passC11 number to F-03.
- The heading says the layer choice moves the result "ten times the claimed effect" and the
  body says "eleven times"; 0.365 / 0.033 = 11.1, 0.365 / 0.037 = 9.9. Both are defensible
  depending on which margin is used; they are not consistent with each other.
