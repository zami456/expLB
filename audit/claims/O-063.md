# O-063 — protocol: the P1 diagonal uses the held-out 25 %, the off-diagonal mean averages all i ≠ j

**Source:** `docs/thesis/persona-coordinates-70b-results.md:222` (§2.9, "P1, pairwise transfer").

**Claim as stated:** "The **diagonal** (i = j) uses the held-out 25 %. The **off-diagonal mean**
averages all i ≠ j."

**Restated:** in protocol P1, a probe trained on dataset *i* is evaluated on dataset *j*. When
*j* = *i* it is evaluated only on the 25 % of the data that was held out of training; when
*j* ≠ *i* it is evaluated on *j*'s whole balanced pool; and the "off-diagonal mean" reported in
every transfer table is the unweighted mean of those *i* ≠ *j* cells.

## Level 1 / Level 2

Both sentences are confirmed by the code *and* reproduced arithmetically: `off_diagonal` equals
the unweighted mean of all 90 *i* ≠ *j* cells to 6 dp in all 8 stored feature/probe rows, and
`diagonal` equals the mean of the 10 *i* = *i* cells. The sibling §2.9 statement "9 labelled
datasets give **72 pairs**; adding `sycophancy_mt` gives **90**" also checks out: this file has
10 labelled datasets (alpaca is single-class and drops out) and n_pairs = 90 = 10 × 9.

## Two precisions that a careful reader should have

1. **The 75/25 split is over `item_id`s, not rows.** `group_split` shuffles items, cuts at
   `round(0.75 · n_items)`, and takes all rows of the chosen items. So "the held-out 25 %" is
   25 % of items; the realised row share differs whenever items have unequal numbers of rows
   (e.g. `ai_liar`: 54 items × 5 samples → 200/70 items but only 20 balanced on-diagonal test
   rows). The doc says "75 % of items" one line earlier, so §2.9 as a whole is correct; the
   sentence in isolation is not.
2. **The diagonal is then also class-balanced and can be very small.** `ai_liar`'s on-diagonal
   test pool is 20 rows; `alpaca`'s is 0. Diagonal AUROCs in this document are therefore much
   noisier than off-diagonal ones — an asymmetry no table states.
