random-direction null.

## Level 2

Not re-derivable from rollouts here (the sleeper activation store is lab/Drive-only). What I did
instead is check the *internal* logic of sub-claim (ii): the doc argues that what is detected is
falsity because (a) the trigger cue is ruled out — cross-trigger 0.9639 ≥ within-organism 0.9625,
so nothing is lost when the trigger type changes — and (b) the answer token is ruled out, because
a probe scored strictly inside one answer token still reads 0.946–0.961 while the token identity
alone reads only 0.617–0.627. Both legs are in the file and both hold.

The natural control also holds: gender-secret, whose lie is not checkable from context, gives
raw corpus→sleeper transfer 0.255–0.660 across the ten sources (mean 0.4726, i.e. chance) while
its within-organism number is 0.997 (recomputed: mean 0.4720, min 0.2551, max 0.6599 — the doc
says "0.255–0.660 across sources", which matches). Two further §7.11 cells also reproduce:
raw corpus→sleeper mean of 10 sources = 0.8534 (doc 0.853) and, excluding the belief sources,
9 sources without `sycophancy_mt` = 0.9315 (doc 0.932).

## Verdict

**HOLDS.** Every number quoted in §1.2 and §7.11 reproduces from the two result files, and the
falsity argument's two legs (cue ruled out, answer token ruled out) are both present in the data.

*Confidence:* HIGH for the numbers; MEDIUM for the *interpretation* ("what they detect is
falsity") — that is an inference from two eliminations, not a positive measurement of falsity.
*Importance:* HIGH — this is the claim the doc itself withdraws ("the zero-shot axis claim is
withdrawn"), and in a methods-and-negative-results framing it is one of the cleanest negatives
in the project.
