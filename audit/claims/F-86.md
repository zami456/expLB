# F-86 — "Why pooling works: single-source directions are nearly orthogonal, and averaging them cancels the noise. Joint training is not needed — and is worse."

**Source:** `kd/docs/FINDINGS.md:3467` (batch P2-pooled). **Importance: HIGH**
(this is "Result 2" of the pooled line of work and the mechanism claim the draft would have to
engage with).

## The claim, restated
At L40, mean pooling, `paper` probe, seed 42, 11 datasets: averaging the ten unit-normalised
single-source probe directions and projecting gives **0.8828**, which beats joint pooled training
(**0.8545**, +0.028), beats budget-matched pooling (**0.8062**, +0.077) and beats single-source
transfer (**0.6763**, +0.207). The single-source directions are nearly orthogonal
(mean pairwise cosine **+0.051**) while the pooled direction sits nearer them (**+0.227**), and
the source-count curve rises and flattens (0.673 / 0.740 / 0.782 / 0.815 / 0.820 / 0.864 / 0.809
for k = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10).

## Three problems, in order of size

**1. The +0.028 margin is smaller than its comparator's own subsampling spread — and F-86 does not
say so.** `30f_result2_audit.py` drew the budget-matched pooled comparator 20 times: mean 0.8344,
**range [0.7912, 0.8736], sd 0.0200, max−min 0.0824**. The script prints the verdict itself:
*"=> the margin is INSIDE the subsampling spread of its own comparator."* That printed sentence
appears nowhere in F-86. (Fairness note: the 0.0824 is the spread of the *budget-matched* arm,
while the +0.028 is measured against the *full* arm, which is not subsampled; the dataset-level
bootstrap on the same margin — stored in `audit_L40_s42.json` — does clear, but only just:
+0.0284, CI **[+0.0045, +0.0523]**, i.e. the lower bound is 0.0045. Either way "beats joint
training" is a knife-edge result, not a comfortable one.) This also supersedes F-86's caveat (3),
"no dataset-level bootstrap on the mean-of-directions number yet" — one exists.

**2. Two different numbers in the ledger carry the same name.** F-86's table row "pooled LODO,
row-budget matched = **0.8062**" comes from `why_L40_s42.json`; F-79/F-80's "pooled LODO,
row-budget matched = **0.8500**" comes from `premise_L40_mean_s42.json`, at the *same* layer,
pooling, probe and seed. The two scripts implement the budget slightly differently and draw from
different RNG states (`30d_why_pooling.py` re-subsamples inside `fit()`; `30_premise.py` draws the
rows once per target). Difference **−0.0438**. Against F-79/F-80's value the mean-of-directions
margin would be **+0.033**, not +0.077. A reader of the ledger cannot tell these apart.

**3. The source-count curve is not monotone, and the non-monotone point is the one the table
omits.** F-86 prints k = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10 and calls the curve "rising and flattening", blaming
the k = 10 dip on the row budget. The file also holds **k = 4 = 0.7178**, which is *below* k = 3's
0.7815 and only just above k = 2's 0.7404. With k = 4 included the curve reads
0.673 / 0.740 / 0.782 / **0.718** / 0.815 / 0.816 / 0.820 / 0.828 / 0.864 / 0.809 — noisy, with a
20-subset-per-size Monte-Carlo error large enough to hide the trend it is offered as evidence for.
Omitting k = 4, 6, 8 from the table and explaining only the k = 10 dip presents the curve as
cleaner than the stored numbers are.

## Verdict
**PARTLY HOLDS.** Every number in the entry reproduces from its file. The geometry result
(pairwise cosine +0.051, pooled-to-single +0.227) and the big margin over single-source transfer
(+0.207) are solid. The headline "**beats** joint training" rests on +0.028, which its own audit
script declared inside the comparator's subsampling spread and whose dataset bootstrap clears with
a lower bound of +0.0045; the "+0.077 over budget-matched pooling" uses a 0.8062 that conflicts
with the 0.8500 the ledger reports for the same quantity elsewhere; and the source-count curve is
presented without its non-monotone point.
**Confidence: HIGH** on the numbers and on the conflict; **MEDIUM** on how much the +0.028 should
be discounted (the 0.0824 spread is not a strictly matched null).
