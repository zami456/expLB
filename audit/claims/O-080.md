# O-080 — "the erased direction … has only cos 0.30–0.37 with the published Assistant Axis"

**Source:** `docs/thesis/persona-coordinates-70b-results.md:677–679`, the "Caveat (this audit)" box
under §7.4 (inventory cites `:672`; +6-line offset, see O-076). Batch **W6B-thesis2**.

## The claim, restated
The direction Q4 projects out is **paper16's own** assistant direction
(`assistant = default − mean(the 25 role personas)`), not Lu et al.'s published Assistant Axis; the
cosine between the two is only **0.30–0.37**. Therefore "signal survives removal of *the* Assistant
Axis" has not been tested.

`UNDERSPECIFIED`: no layers are named. I tested three readings — the three layers Q4 actually erases
at (20, 40, 44), the five layers the thesis works at (20, 22, 40, 44, 60), and the whole L20–L60
band.

## What I did
`audit/scripts/claims/W6B_geom.py`:
- loads `run-hf275/published_assistant_axis.pt` (80 × 8192);
- takes the **old** paper16 assistant axis from `run-paper16/axes_vectors_s3.pt`;
- **rebuilds** the **fp32** paper16 assistant direction from the 26 cached per-persona vectors
  (`audit/cache/labpc/run-paper16/vectors_s3_fp32/`) with `6_axes.py`'s construction, because the
  fp32 bank file itself is lab-only and the Q4 script defaults to it;
- prints cos at every one of the 80 layers.
Sanity check first: rebuilding the **old** bank from `run-paper16/vectors_s3/` reproduces its stored
assistant axis at min cos 1.000000 over all 80 layers, so the construction is right.

## Level 1 / Level 2
There is no result file holding this cosine — it is prose in the caveat box and in the memory note
`paper16-assistant-direction-not-lu-axis`. So this is a **Level-2-only** check, computed from the
vector stores.

| reading | measured range | matches "0.30–0.37"? |
|---|---|---|
| the five thesis layers 20/22/40/44/60 | 0.2997 – 0.3719 | **yes, exactly** |
| the three Q4 erasure layers 20/40/44 | 0.3565 – 0.3719 | yes (inside) |
| the whole L20–L60 band, 41 layers | 0.2601 – 0.4212 | **no** — wider at both ends |
| all 80 layers | 0.2601 – 0.6590 | no (early layers reach 0.66) |

## Version
- Published axis: `run-hf275/published_assistant_axis.pt`, md5 `2807d7caa7f5f0126be9ba4c4b6ea906`.
- paper16 old bank: md5 `5d05a42542ae13fc7ac5d893a4ae4f8c` (RF-15 "old"), laptop = Drive.
- paper16 fp32: rebuilt from the audit's own cached copy of the lab `vectors_s3_fp32`.
- **RF-15 is immaterial here**: old vs fp32 differ by at most 0.0008 in this cosine and their
  assistant directions have cos ≥ 0.999959 at every layer. So which bank Q4 used does not change
  the answer.

## Robustness
The substantive point is robust well beyond the quoted bracket: across the entire L20–L60 band the
cosine never exceeds **0.42**, i.e. the two directions share at most ~18 % of their variance
anywhere in the range the thesis uses. The claim would be even stronger stated as a band maximum.

## Verdict — **HOLDS**
The bracket reproduces exactly at the layers the document works at, and the conclusion it supports
is true everywhere in the band.

**Confidence:** HIGH — computed from the raw per-persona vectors, with a construction sanity check
that returns cos 1.000000 against the stored bank.
**Importance:** HIGH — it is the caveat that decides whether §7.4 may be written as a result about
"the Assistant Axis". It may not.

**One precision for the thesis:** "0.30–0.37" is a five-layer sample, not a band range. Over
L20–L60 it is **0.26–0.42**. Say which layers, or quote the band.
