## The claim, in one plain sentence
Of the 1,350 paper16 persona rollouts, the 70B judge gives the top score (3) to **80.7 %** and the
405B judge to **79.9 %** — a **0.8 pp** gap — and that score-3 set is what the persona vectors are
built from.

80.67 % and 79.93 % round to **80.7 %** and **79.9 %** exactly as stated. The difference is
**0.74 pp**, which the note rounds to 0.8 pp (it is also the difference of the rounded figures,
0.8, so the phrasing is defensible either way; the exact value is 0.74).

## The second half of the sentence — "score-3 is the filter that builds the vectors"
**Verified in code.** `src/assistant_axis_ndif/2_activations_ndif.py:20-21` states
`"Kept" = judge score == 3 (--keep_scores), except `default` where all responses are used`, and
line 494 sets `--keep_scores` default `"3"`; line 449 turns it into the keep-set. That is exactly
the filter the note names, and it also explains the `s3` / `all` bank pair
(`vectors_s3` vs `vectors_all`, `axes_vectors_s3.pt` vs `axes_vectors_all.pt`) — the `all` arm is
the unfiltered control. F-65's audit check measured the `s3`-vs-`all` gap on the cap bank at
≤0.014. So the filter claim is supported and its practical consequence — a 0.74 pp shift in the
score-3 rate can move the vectors only slightly — is the point the note makes.
