## The claim, in one plain sentence
The L2 norm of the paper16 `deception` axis stored in `run-paper16/axes_vectors_s3.pt` rises from
**0.025 at layer 0 to 5.386 at layer 76**, so any projection onto it must normalise the direction
first or the layer sweep measures the norm rather than the direction.

## Verdict — **HOLDS**
Both quoted numbers are exact, on both vector versions, on both machines.

**Two things the note's wording hides** (not errors, but a future session should know):
1. The bank stores **80 layers, 0–79**, and the norm keeps climbing past the quoted endpoint —
   **7.03 at L79**. "0.025 → 5.386 from layer 0 to 76" is a window, not the range.
2. The same is true of the other three axes, and `sycophancy` grows further still
   (0.033 → 8.37). The methodological instruction ("normalise first") therefore applies bank-wide,
   which is what the code does (`unit()` in `9_persona_probes.py`).

**Confidence:** HIGH — read straight from the stored tensors with `torch`, twice, on two copies.
**Importance:** LOW-MEDIUM — it is a methods caveat, not a result; but it is the justification for
normalising in every projection script, so it is worth being right.
