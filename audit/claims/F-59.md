# F-59 — "HP-KR growth GATE FAILED — stated knowledge tracks confidence, not correctness"

**Batch:** P6-kd-rest · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The claim, restated
A pre-registered gate ("denial rate ≥ 0.70") on three routes for growing the HP-KR
knowledge-matched slice, each 20 WMDP items put to Llama-3.3-70B through NDIF:

| route | selection | template | denial rate | verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| primary | `correct == 0` (312 items available) | HP-KR harmful | **5/20 = 0.250** | GATE FAILED |
| fallback 1 | `correct == 0` | HP-KR control | **3/20 = 0.150** | GATE FAILED |
| fallback 2 | lowest softmax `margin` | HP-KR harmful | **5/12 = 0.417** | MARGINAL |

Interpretation: the model claims to know answers it gets wrong on **75%** of the items it
answers incorrectly; the neutral template elicits *more* claimed knowledge, not less; and
selecting on the model's own confidence nearly doubles the denial rate — so stated knowledge
tracks confidence, not accuracy. Also incidental: **8 of 20** margin-selected items were lost
to NDIF OOM. Caveat: n = 20 per route (12 for fallback 2), so ±0.10.

Source: `kd/docs/FINDINGS.md:1829-1863`, citing `logs/hpkr_denial_{smoke,control,margin}.log`
and `kd/run/denial/*.jsonl`.

## Robustness — the power check the entry does not do
The entry's own caveat is the binding one: 20 items per route (12 after OOM). It quotes
"roughly ±0.10", which is about one standard error, not an interval.
`audit/scripts/claims/F-59_power.py` computes exact binomial intervals and pairwise tests:

```
  primary  (wrong, harmful)    5/20 = 0.250   exact 95% CI [0.087, 0.491]
  fallback1(wrong, control)    3/20 = 0.150   exact 95% CI [0.032, 0.379]
  fallback2(margin, harmful)   5/12 = 0.417   exact 95% CI [0.152, 0.723]

the gate was 'denial rate >= 0.70':
  primary    upper 95% bound 0.491 -> gate EXCLUDED
  fallback1  upper 95% bound 0.379 -> gate EXCLUDED
  fallback2  upper 95% bound 0.723 -> gate not excluded

Fisher exact, two-sided:
  primary 5/20 vs fallback1 3/20 : p = 0.6948
  primary 5/20 vs fallback2 5/12 : p = 0.4382
  fallback1 3/20 vs fallback2 5/12: p = 0.1161
```

So: the **gate verdict is solid** for the two `correct == 0` routes (0.70 is outside both
intervals), and F-59's own "MARGINAL" label for fallback 2 is exactly right — its interval
still contains 0.70. But the two directional statements the interpretation rests on —
"fallback 1 made it worse (0.250 → 0.150)" and "fallback 2 nearly doubles it" — are **not
distinguishable at this n** (p = 0.69 and p = 0.44). The headline reading, *stated knowledge
tracks confidence rather than accuracy*, is supported by the composition of the fallback-2
sample (3 of its 12 items were answered correctly, margins 0.10–0.35, vs margins 0.95–1.00 in
the primary route) more than by the difference between the rates.

No layer, bank, pooling or seed enters this claim — it is a generation-side measurement.

## Verdict — **HOLDS** (gate failed, as stated)
Confidence **HIGH** for the three rates, the 312, the 75% and the OOM count: every one
recomputed from the raw rollouts and cross-read in the GATE lines.
**MEDIUM** for the interpretive sentences ("fallback 1 made it worse", "fallback 2 nearly
doubles it") — the direction is right but the sample cannot separate these rates.
Importance **MEDIUM-HIGH** — it is why route (b) for growing the matched slice was
deprioritised, and it supplies the mechanism behind F-08's 24% belief/accuracy disagreement,
which is the heart of the HP-KR confound.
