## The claim in one sentence

Across six held-out datasets and two depths, the fixed label-free persona direction never drops
below AUROC 0.64 (layer 20) or 0.71 (layer 40), whereas probes trained on pooled labelled data fall
to 0.12 and 0.49 -- so the defensible claim is about robustness, not peak accuracy.

## Two things the sentence gets wrong

1. **"six held-out Liars' Bench datasets" -- only five are Liars' Bench.** The sixth is
   `repe_honesty__plain`, the RepE honesty set the base probes are trained on. His own comparison
   table uses `n_datasets = 5` and labels the five-dataset mean "Average (Liars' Bench)", so the
   distinction is drawn elsewhere in his own files.
2. **The pooled floors quoted are the balanced pooling at both depths**, while H-010 two paragraphs
   earlier quotes the *naive* pooling at L40 (0.523). Under naive the L40 pooled floor is 0.5230,
   not 0.49. Neither choice is flagged. Taking the better pooling at each depth (as H-009 does), the
   pooled floors would be 0.1243 (L20, balanced) and 0.5230 (L40, naive).

## Robustness

- Two depths only (20, 40); one fit per cell, no seeds.
- The persona axis has no training set, so "generalization gap is zero by construction" is a
  definition, not a measurement -- correctly stated as such.
- His `loo_bootstrap.csv` shows the pooled baseline **beating** the persona axis on CG at both
  depths and on HP-C at L20, so "never fails catastrophically" is about the floor, not about
  winning; the note says this.

## Verdict

**PARTLY MATCHES HIS DATA.** All four numbers are exact and Level-2 reproducible. The framing is
loose in two ways: one of the six datasets is not a Liars' Bench dataset, and the pooled floors
silently use a different pooling than the immediately preceding paragraph.

Confidence HIGH. Importance HIGH -- this block quote is written as the paper-ready sentence, so the
mislabel would travel into the paper verbatim.
