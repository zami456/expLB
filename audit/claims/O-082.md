## Two things the doc's cross-reference does not carry
1. **10 datasets, 3,624 rows — not "400 rows per dataset".** §7.12's parenthesis says
   "(`validate_L40.json`, L40 mean, **400 rows per dataset**)". 400 is the `--cap`
   (`17_validate.py:59`), an upper bound; the realised run had **3,624 rows over 10 datasets**
   (average 362), and the dataset-accuracy figure is computed only over datasets with ≥100 rows
   ("over 10 datasets" in the log). Minor, but it is the kind of number a reviewer recomputes.
2. **The §7.5 sentence borrows a 10-dataset measurement into a 9/11-dataset argument.** §7.5's
   within/across-construct means are over the Q5 pair matrix; §7.12's decodability is over the
   Pass-B pooled corpus at L40. The inference ("a high within-construct mean is expected under
   either hypothesis") is qualitative and survives, but the two numbers are not from the same
   row set and the doc does not say so.

Bonus cross-check, useful elsewhere: the same log gives `cos(deception, assistant) = −0.371`,
which is the paper16 contamination figure the owner's memory note quotes as −0.37.

## Robustness
One layer (L40), one pooling (mean), one seed (`default_rng(0)` for the per-dataset subsample),
3-fold CV. No `validate_L20.json` / `validate_L44.json` exists on the lab PC, so the decodability
gap is not depth-checked. The claim does not assert depth-robustness.
