# F-21 — "NDIF small-model deployments cap memory PER PROCESS, not per GPU"

**Source:** `kd/docs/FINDINGS.md:365` (2026-09-20). Batch W5A-ledger.

## The claim, restated
3B/8B generation failed with CUDA OOM while the GPU was 83% free, because NDIF caps memory per
process at ~7.37 GiB. Quoted block: `44.42 GiB total, 36.73 GiB FREE / this process: 7.69 GiB in use
-- "7.37 GiB allowed"`. Allocation scales linearly with `--gen_batch`: 96 → 140 MiB (fails),
24 → 38 MiB (fails), **8 → 14 MiB (84/100 rows with retries disabled)**. Settled on
`--gen_batch 8 --session_batches 4 --retries 6`.

## Verdict — PARTLY HOLDS
The headline — a **per-process** cap of 7.37 GiB on a GPU that is 82.7% free — is verified verbatim
in the cited log, as is the settled configuration. **The three-row gen_batch sweep has no raw data.**
`run-scale/smoke3b` exists neither on the lab PC nor on Drive; the 3B generate log contains
allocations of 36–142 MiB only, never 14 or 38 MiB; and the table itself is a word-for-word copy of
a comment the owner wrote in `scale/scripts/run_scale_chain.ps1:149`. It is the same author's prose,
not independent evidence, and the "84/100 rows with retries disabled" cell is unverifiable.

**Confidence HIGH** (the log is unambiguous; the absence of the sweep store is confirmed against
both the lab census and Drive). **Importance LOW** (engineering; it constrains the 3B/8B rungs'
throughput but no scientific number depends on it).
