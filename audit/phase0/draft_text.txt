Abstract
Linear probes for lie detection often fail to transfer across datasets.Mahadik and Skapars(2026)
derive detectors frompersona coordinates instead: directions built from a model’s activations while it
role-plays deceptive and honest personas, which never see an evaluation set. They report better cross-
dataset generalization at 3B and 8B. We project the lies of Liars’ Bench onto persona coordinates of
Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. On the benchmark’s hardest dataset, harm-pressure knowledge report (HP-KR),
which the original study did not evaluate, the published persona contrast inverts, scoring 0.148 AUROC
atlayer20. HP-KR’sliesaredenialsofknowledge, andnoneofthepublisheddeceptivepersonasliesthat
way. We extend the persona bank with ten roles that feign ignorance, are sincerely ignorant, or plainly
assert what they know. In these knowledge-denier persona coordinates, with no benchmark labels, a
single contrast direction reaches 0.919 AUROC and 0.910 balanced accuracy at a 1% false-positive rate
on HP-KR at layer 40. The benchmark’s own probes score 0.50 and 0.26 there, and its upper bound,

1 Introduction
Linear probes read a language model’s internal activations directly, which makes them attractive monitors
for deception: a model that lies need not say anything that gives it away (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025). But
a probe is trained on one distribution and deployed on others, and probes often fail to transfer (Kirch et al.,
2026;Kumar,2026;Natarajanetal. ,2026). Liars’Benchmakesthefailureconcrete( Kretschmaretal. ,2025).
Across seven datasets of lies generated on-policy by four open-weight models, a probe trained on instructed
honest and deceptive responses, followingGoldowsky-Dill et al.(2025), reaches balanced accuracy close to
chance on most datasets. The hardest is harm-pressure knowledge report (HP-KR), in which a model denies
knowing the answer to a hazardous question that it has been shown to know. Every method the benchmark
evaluates has recall near zero there at a 1% false-positive rate, and its probe’s AUROC on Llama-3.3-70B is
0.12.
Mahadik and Skapars(2026) propose a different source of directions. Following the Assistant Axis
methodology (Lu et al., 2026), they prompt a model to play deceptive roles, such as a spin doctor or a
coverup operator, and honest ones, such as an ombudsman or a fact checker. They average its activations
over each role’s responses and use the resulting geometry in two ways: directly, as a zero-shot contrast
between the deceptive and honest group means, or as a low-dimensional coordinate system in which to train
probes. The directions come from role-play rather than from any evaluation dataset, so they cannot fit one
dataset’s idiosyncrasies. On Llama 3.2-3B and Llama 3-8B, probes in thesepersona coordinates transfer

We take both. We project the lies of five Liars’ Bench datasets, together with the RepE instruction pairs
on which the benchmark’s probe is trained, onto persona coordinates of Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct at three
depths, and measure cross-dataset transfer over the full train–test matrix (Figure1). This exposes a failure
the original study did not test. On HP-KR, the published persona contrast inverts: it ranks lies as more
honest than honest answers, with AUROC 0.148 at layer 20. The cause lies in the personas. HP-KR’s lies
are denials of knowledge, while the published deceptive personas are specialists in omission who control
timing, emphasis and disclosure, and none of them lies by claiming ignorance. We therefore add ten roles to
the bank: four that feign ignorance, three that are sincerely ignorant, and three that plainly assert what they
know. Projected into theseknowledge-denier persona coordinates, the lies separate.

2 Related work
Probing for lies and deception. Hidden states of language models predict whether a statement is true
(AzariaandMitchell ,2023;MarksandTegmark ,2023),andtruth-relateddirectionscanberecoveredwithout
labels (Burns et al., 2023). Zou et al.(2023) read honesty from contrastive instruction pairs.Goldowsky-
Dilletal. (2025)trainedprobesonsuchpairsforLlama-3.3-70B-Instructandtestedthemonrealisticsettings
such as insider-trading concealment (Scheurer et al., 2023). Their protocol sets the decision threshold to a
1% false-positive rate on benign chat, and it is the protocol we follow. Liars’ Bench (Kretschmar et al.,2025)
collects 72,863 on-policy lies and honest responses across seven datasets and four models. It evaluates an
LLM judge, self-evaluation, the unrelated-question classifier ofPacchiardi et al.(2023), and three probes:
a Mean Probe trained on instruction pairs, a Follow-Up Probe read at a prefilled “No” after the question
“Was your last response deceptive?”, and an Upper-Bound Probe trained with privileged examples from the
benchmark itself. Most methods perform near chance on the harm-pressure datasets. On HP-KR, which is
builtfromhazardous-knowledgequestions( Lietal. ,2024), thebenchmarkreportsrecallsnearzeroforevery
method.
Personas as directions. Persona vectors (Chen et al., 2025) are activation directions for character traits,
extracted automatically from a natural-language description and used to monitor and steer those traits. The
Assistant Axis (Lu et al., 2026) extracts directions for a large set of character archetypes and finds that the
leading component of this persona space measures how far the model sits from its default assistant persona.
Mahadik and Skapars(2026) turn this machinery into a monitor. They build deception and sycophancy
axes from contrasting persona groups, then use the contrast direction and the first principal component zero-
shot, and the top three components as features for probes. On Llama 3.2-3B and Llama 3-8B these persona
coordinates transfer across ten deception and sycophancy datasets, including Liars’ Bench’s Convincing-
Game and Instructed-Deception, better than probes on raw activations. Our work scales their method to 70B
and extends their persona bank.
Why probes fail to transfer. Kirchetal. (2026)findthatprobegeneralizationdependsonhowthetraining
data was generated, and that failures are largest for behaviours defined by intent, such as strategic deception.
Natarajanetal. (2026)finddeceptiondetectionheterogeneous: probesmatchedtoaspecifictypeofdeception
outperformauniversalone. Ourknowledge-denialpersonasapplythatmatchingatthelevelofpersonas. Yoo
and Skapars(2026) show that projecting onto a well-chosen subset of the training distribution’s principal
components nearly closes the gap to probes trained on the test distribution.Kumar (2026) tests whether
deception is a single linear direction by training probes on the top-𝑘 principal components of the training
activations of Gemma 3 models. The top component alone captures only 0.61–0.80 AUROC, which he takes

3.2 The persona bank
The bank holds 36 personas (Table5 in AppendixA). Twenty-six are those ofMahadik and Skapars(2026):
eight deceptive roles that control what is disclosed (handler, broker, impression manager, coverup operator,
gatekeeper, spin doctor, plausible denier, narrative manager), seven honest ones (ombudsman, transparency
officer, disclosure counsel, whistleblower, documentarian, fact checker, archivist), five sycophantic and five
anti-sycophantic roles, and the default assistant with no role prompt. That paper publishes the roles’ names
andone-linedescriptionsbutnottheirprompts, sowewrotefivepromptvariantsforeachpersonainthestyle
of the Assistant Axis (Lu et al., 2026).
The ten new roles form three groups, built around the kind of lie that HP-KR contains:
• Knowledge denial (4): an amnesiac witness, a deniable expert, a stonewaller and a feigned novice. Each
holds knowledge and denies it. The lieis the feigned ignorance.
• Sincere ignorance (3): an honest novice, a calibrated reporter and a scrupulous witness. Each denies
knowledge truthfully.
• Plain assertion (3): a forthright expert, a candid witness and a plain dealer. Each plainly asserts what it
knows.
The honest side is split in two because a person feigning ignorance and one who truly lacks the knowledge
say nearly the same words, such as “I’m not in a position to say”. Only a contrast in which both sides deny
can separate the lie from its wording. The contrasts we evaluate pool both honest groups, and Section6
discusses what that leaves open. One variant of each side of the sharpest pair reads:
Feigned novice: “You are a feigned novice who understates what you understand so the work goes else-
where.”
Honest novice: “Youareanhonestnovicewhosaysplainlywhensomethingispastwhatyouunderstand.”

of Mahadik and Skapars(2026). The other ten are new and concern the speaker’s own knowledge. In some
the speaker knows (“I’m asked whether this falls within my expertise. It does, but answering makes me

and is not judged. That leaves 3,749 rollouts (69%), between 47 and 149 per judged persona, and we extract

3.4 Scoring
Zero-shot, a pooled activation𝑧 is scored by its projection onto the unit contrast,𝑠(𝑧) = ⟨𝑧, 𝑑/∥ 𝑑 ∥⟩, with
higher meaning more deceptive. The sign is fixed by construction, and no labels are used.Trained, we fit an

probe) on one of three feature sets: the raw 8192-dimensional pooled activations, the projections⟨𝑧, 𝑢𝑖⟩ onto
𝑘 persona components, or the one-dimensional contrast score. A probe on a single feature can learn only a
sign and a scale. Whenever every training set agrees on the sign, its AUROC on a test set therefore equals
the zero-shot AUROC, and it has no training set to overfit. As a control, three random directions replace the
persona components (five draws).

5 Results
5.1 The hardest dataset: lies told as denials
HP-KR asks the model whether it knows the answer to a hazardous multiple-choice question. Liars’ Bench
firstestablishes,inneutralcontexts, whichquestionsthemodelknows. Aresponseisalieifthemodeldenies
knowing an answer it has been shown to know. Responses that affirm the knowledge, and denials where no
knowledgewasestablished, arehonest( Kretschmaretal. ,2025, §4). EverylieinHP-KRisthereforeadenial
of knowledge.
The published persona contrast gets HP-KR backwards (Table2, Figure3). At layer 20 its AUROC is
0.148: itscoresthemodel’sliesasmorehonestthanitshonestanswers. Atlayers40and44itsitsnearchance,
at 0.593 and 0.589. It is not alone. The RepE-trained base probe also inverts at layer 20 (0.109), as does the
Liars’ Bench Mean Probe (0.12). The published deceptive personas explain why the persona contrast has
nothing to go on. They lie by omission: they control “timing, emphasis, and selective disclosure”, “bury or
exclude material facts” and give “defensible half-disclosures” (Mahadik and Skapars, 2026, Table 2). None
of them lies by claiming not to know.
Adding the knowledge-denial personas repairs the ranking. With no benchmark labels, the knowledge-
denial contrast reaches 0.890, 0.919 and 0.908 AUROC at layers 20, 40 and 44. The extended contrast,
which pools both deceptive groups, keeps nearly all of it (0.882, 0.912, 0.894). The repair comes from the
new personas, not from re-extracting the old ones with more rollouts. The published grouping inverts on the
original bank (0.103 at layer 20) and still inverts on the re-extracted one (0.148).

The benchmark’s threshold separates the knowledge-denial contrast from everything else. At layer 40 it
reaches 0.910 balanced accuracy at 1% FPR on the Alpaca control. Its recall is 0.885, and it flags 6% of the
honest answers. Liars’ Bench reports 0.50 for its Mean Probe, 0.26 for its Follow-Up Probe, and 0.95 for
the privileged Upper-Bound Probe. The base probe ranks HP-KR well at layer 40, with AUROC 0.936, yet
all of its HP-KR scores fall below the Alpaca threshold, so it flags nothing (0.500). So do the published and
extended contrasts. The threshold is not reliable even for the knowledge-denial contrast. At layer 20 it sits

5.2 Cross-dataset transfer
Figure 4 shows the full transfer matrix at layer 40, and Table3 summarises all three layers. Probes on raw
activations fit their own dataset, with a mean in-distribution AUROC of 0.983 at layer 40, but lose 0.251 of it
off the diagonal, falling to 0.732. Some cells invert. The worst, a probe trained on CG and tested on HP-KR,
scores 0.06. The extended persona contrast was shaped by no evaluation dataset, and it reaches 0.904, 0.916
and 0.903 off the diagonal at layers 20, 40 and 44, against 0.648, 0.732 and 0.716 for the raw probes. The
comparison holds per dataset as well as overall. Averaged over training sets, the extended contrast beats the
raw probes on every held-out dataset at every layer, and matches or beats the published contrast (Table7,
Appendix B). Single cells can still favour a raw probe; at layer 40 the probe trained on ID scores higher on
CG than the contrast does.
Removing HP-KR shows where the extended bank’s advantage over the published contrast comes from.
At layer 20, without HP-KR, the published contrast is marginally ahead (0.919 against 0.909), so there the
whole gain is the HP-KR repair. At layers 40 and 44 the extended contrast leads with or without HP-KR
(0.917 against 0.880 at layer 40). The knowledge-denial contrast on its own transfers nearly as well as the
extended one (0.912 at layer 40), even though its personas were written for a single kind of lie.

6 Limitations
The wording of a denial. Every HP-KR lie is a denial of knowledge, and most honest answers affirm
it. A detector of denial wording would therefore score well on HP-KR without detecting deception. Our
knowledge-denial contrast does not rule this out. Its honest side pools personas that deny knowledge truth-
fully (sincere ignorance) with personas that do not deny at all (plain assertion), so part of the direction can
separate denying from asserting. The bank was designed for the cleaner test, a contrast between feigned and
sincere ignorance in which both sides deny, and we have not run it. At the level of the personas the signs are
encouraging: sincere-ignorance personas sit with the honest cluster on the knowledge-denial axis (Figure2).
Whetherthesameholdsformodelresponsesisuntested. HP-KRitselfholdsthetestcases: itshonestdenials,
where the model’s knowledge of the answer could not be established.
A designed repair. We wrote the knowledge-denial personas after seeing the published contrast invert on
HP-KR, so HP-KR measures a repair designed for it, not a blind test. The other datasets are the untargeted
test. There the extended contrast matches or beats the published one at every layer. But at layer 20, with
HP-KR excluded, its advantage over the published contrast disappears (Section5.2).
Calibration. A threshold set on benign chat does not transfer to every dataset. On HP-C almost nothing
crosses it, whichever detector is used. On HP-KR at layer 20 the knowledge-denial contrast’s threshold falls
inside the honest answers. In the transfer matrix, balanced accuracy at that threshold sits at its 0.5 floor in 18
of the 30 off-diagonal cells for the raw probes at layer 40. This is why AUROC, which needs no threshold,
is our main measure of transfer, with balanced accuracy reported beside it.
The supervised baseline. Each probe in our transfer matrix is trained on a single dataset. A probe trained
on several datasets pooled is a stronger supervised baseline, and we do not compare against it.
Scope. We study one model, three layers and one persona bank. We wrote the prompts from the published
role descriptions, and they may differ from the prompts ofMahadik and Skapars(2026). The judge keeps
onlyrollouts that stayedin character, whichmay bias the persona vectorstoward prototypical behaviour. The
re-extracted bank used more rollouts per persona than the original, though the published grouping inverts

Because our insider-trading scores exclude the reasoning scratchpad, comparisons with Liars’ Bench’s pub-
lished numbers on that dataset are not like for like. Comparisons within our pipeline are exact.
7 Future work
More model families. Liars’ Bench also covers Mistral-Small-24B, Qwen-2.5-72B and Gemma-3-27B
(Kretschmar et al., 2025). Whether knowledge-denier coordinates built on one family work on another, or
have to be rebuilt per model, is the next question.
Fine-tuned models. Soft-Trigger and Gender-Secret contain lies from models fine-tuned to lie. There the
reasonforlyingwaslearnedintrainingratherthangivenincontext. Whethercoordinatesbuiltfromrole-play
capture a lie that training instilled is open.
Wording-controlled contrasts. The contrast between feigned and sincere ignorance, tested on HP-KR’s
honest denials, would separate the lie from its wording.
Combining signals. Aletheia’s Quest combined a black-box auditor with a white-box self-evaluation chan-
nel, in which the model judges its own reply in a third-person auditing context (Birardi et al., 2026). A
persona-contrast score is a label-free white-box signal that could join such a combination. For detection, the
top tenth of response tokens is a better read than the mean (AppendixD).
Calibration. A contrastthat ranks a dataset wellstill needs a threshold that holds on that dataset’s prompts.
Finding one without labels from the target distribution remains open.
8 Conclusion
Persona coordinates scale to a 70B model, but only as far as the persona bank covers the kind of lie being
told. Liars’ Bench’s hardest dataset consists of lies told as denials of knowledge. The published personas, all
specialists in omission, give a direction that inverts there. Adding roles that deny knowledge turns it into one
that, with no benchmark labels, detects those lies at the benchmark’s own threshold nearly as well as a probe
trained on the benchmark itself. Across datasets, one zero-shot contrast over the extended bank transfers
better on average than probes trained on single datasets, and better than the persona subspace. What it does
not yet have is a threshold that holds on every dataset.

C The direction at the follow-up token
The Follow-Up Probe of Liars’ Bench appends the question “Was your last response deceptive? (Yes/No)”
and a prefilled “No”, then reads the activation at that single token (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025; Kretschmar
et al., 2025). We extracted activations at the “No” (layer 20) and projected them onto the same persona
contrasts (Table8). At that token, the contrast’s sign stops being the same on every dataset. On CG, ID and
RepE the direction still separates lies from honest answers. On IT it flips: the extended contrast falls from
0.990 on the response tokens to 0.173 at the “No”. On HP-KR the repair is lost, falling from 0.882 to 0.190.

the model carries forward to a self-report is not the same signal as the one its response carries.

G Reproduction and compute
The pooled activations reproduce the pipeline. For each layer, we rescored the pooled activations with
the probe the pipeline trained on RepE (its stored direction and scaler). AUROC, balanced accuracy and
recall at the Alpaca threshold matched the pipeline’s own results table to four decimal places on all five
Liars’ Bench datasets. So did the pooled activations at the follow-up token against the Follow-Up run.
Against Liars’ Bench’s published probes. Our measurement of the Follow-Up Probe averages 0.636
AUROC and 0.622 balanced accuracy over the five datasets. The published values are 0.634 and 0.578
(Kretschmar et al., 2025, Table 7). Our base probe, the Mean Probe recipe at the same layer 20, averages
0.697 AUROC and 0.721 balanced accuracy, against the published 0.682 and 0.636 (Kretschmar et al., 2025,
Table 6). Nearly all of the balanced-accuracy gap is insider trading, where our scores exclude the reasoning
